HOW THE "BODY FAILS AFTER HEADERS" CASE IS PRODUCED, AND WHY NOT BY TRUNCATION
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Two mechanisms were measured in this environment before one was chosen. A probe page fetched each
from Chromium and reported which promise rejected:

  /truncate  (200, Content-Length: 4096, a few bytes written, socket destroyed)
             -> {"stage":"fetch-rejected","name":"TypeError","message":"Failed to fetch"}

  /badgzip   (200, Content-Encoding: gzip, body that is not gzip)
             -> {"stage":"body-rejected","status":200,"name":"TypeError","message":"Failed to fetch"}

Destroying the socket makes Chromium fail the whole request: fetch() itself rejects, so no response
reaches the page and NetworkError is the *correct* classification for it. That is not the case the
audit described and it is not used. The malformed content-encoding is: fetch() resolves with a 200
and response.text() rejects, which is a body failure after the headers arrived, and it turns on
malformed content rather than on when a socket closes, so it is deterministic.
